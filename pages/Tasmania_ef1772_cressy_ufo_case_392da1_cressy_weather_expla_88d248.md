## Introduction

A weather-based explanation is one of the strongest conventional interpretations of the 1960 [Cressy]({{ 'cressy/' | relative_url }}) sighting. Lionel and Mary Browning observed the reported cigar-shaped object beside a rain squall, beneath low cloud, with changing sunlight near sunset and a rainbow visible in the same part of the sky. Those are precisely the kinds of conditions in which cloud fragments, rain curtains and shifting illumination can create striking but short-lived visual impressions. At the same time, the Brownings described features that are not easily reproduced by ordinary weather alone, including apparent structure, coordinated movement and several smaller objects interacting with the larger one. The result is not a simple debunking. Instead, the weather hypothesis explains some important aspects of the observation while leaving others open to debate.

## Could a Rain Squall Create the Cressy UFO?

The sighting took place during unsettled weather over northern [Tasmania]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-tasmanias-ufo/' | relative_url }}). Contemporary meteorological summaries describe a trough and frontal system affecting the region, with showers, low cloud, local thunderstorm activity near the Western Tiers and changing light as the sun approached the horizon. The Brownings themselves consistently stated that rain was still falling to the east while the setting sun behind them illuminated parts of the landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1960-the-cressy-cigar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1960 the cressy cigar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1960: The Cressy CigarJuly 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2013</span></span></span>

Those conditions are important because they make judging distance exceptionally difficult. A detached fragment of low cloud, a curtain of rain or an uneven cloud edge can appear surprisingly solid when illuminated from a low angle. Without reliable distance cues, observers may also overestimate or underestimate the size and speed of features suspended against the sky.

The combination of rain ahead and sunlight behind also explains why the [witnesses]({{ 'witnesses-a6fc79/' | relative_url }}) were already looking towards the weather system: they had noticed the rainbow and the dramatic contrast between sunshine and showers before the unusual object attracted their attention. Rainbows themselves require exactly this geometry, with the Sun behind the observer and water droplets ahead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/optical-effects/rainbows" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: weather.metoffice.gov.uk">[Met Office]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">weather.metoffice.gov.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Met Office Rainbows: optical wonders</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Met OfficeRainbows: optical wonders - Met Office...</span></span></span>

## How Scud Cloud Can Mimic Solid Objects

Scud clouds are irregular fragments of low cloud that form beneath larger rain clouds. They often develop rapidly around rain showers and thunderstorm outflows, changing shape from minute to minute.

Several characteristics make scud a plausible ingredient in the Cressy observation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Sharp outlines:** A detached scud fragment can have a surprisingly well-defined edge, particularly when viewed against brighter sky behind it.
* **Elongated forms:** Wind shear may stretch cloud fragments into horizontal or cigar-like shapes.
* **Changing contrast:** Low-angle sunlight can make parts of a cloud appear metallic grey while leaving darker bands where thickness varies.
* **Apparent hovering:** If a cloud is moving largely towards or away from an observer, its sideways motion may appear very slow even though the surrounding air is moving normally.
* **Sudden disappearance:** As rain intensity changes or a cloud fragment merges back into surrounding precipitation, an apparently isolated object can vanish quickly.

</div>

Rain curtains and virgaprecipitation that evaporates before reaching the groundcan also produce sharply defined vertical textures and moving veils that alter an observer's perception of shape and distance. These effects are especially noticeable when illuminated by a low evening sun.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theguardian.com/news/2022/nov/25/weatherwatch-virga-cloud-wispy-curtain-illusion-rain" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theguardian.com">[The Guardian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theguardian.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The GuardianWeatherwatch: Virga clouds wispy curtain is an illusion of rain &#124; Environment &#124; The GuardianNovember 25, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 25, 2022</span></span></span>

Taken together, these mechanisms provide a physically realistic explanation for why an observer might perceive a structured object emerging briefly from a rain squall before disappearing back into it.

## Reported Details the Theory Struggles to Explain

Although the weather explanation fits the setting well, it does not account neatly for every element reported by the Brownings.

The largest difficulty concerns the smaller objects. Lionel Browning described several disc-like forms that appeared near one end of the larger object, while two more reportedly approached rapidly from different directions before the group seemed to reunite and retreat into the rain. He compared their motion to stones skipping across water.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2016/11/the-cressy-tasmanian-sighting-1960.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: The Cressy, Tasmanian sighting - 1960...</span></span></span>

A single cloud fragment cannot easily explain this coordinated pattern. One possibility is that changing illumination revealed multiple separate cloud fragments in quick succession, with the human visual system linking them into coherent moving objects. Another is that rapidly shifting gaps within rain curtains created fleeting bright and dark patches that appeared to travel independently. However, neither explanation reproduces the reported behaviour particularly well.

The Brownings also reported apparent structural details on the larger object, including several dark vertical bands and a short projection resembling an aerial. Cloud edges can contain alternating light and dark regions, but producing repeated, apparently organised markings is more difficult than explaining the object's overall elongated form.

Finally, Browning believed he could see the object against hills beyond the rain, leading him to estimate a relatively nearby distance. If that judgement was correct, an ordinary cloud fragment would become less convincing. If, however, the distance estimate was mistakenas is common in low-contrast weatherthen the apparent size and movement become much easier to reconcile with atmospheric phenomena.

## Why Investigators Reached Different Conclusions

The Royal Australian Air Force ultimately recorded an astronomical explanation involving the rising Moon illuminating scud cloud associated with the rain squall, rather than concluding that an unidentified craft had been present. The weather context therefore formed part of the official interpretation from an early stage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1960-the-cressy-cigar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1960 the cressy cigar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1960: The Cressy CigarJuly 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2013</span></span></span>

Browning disagreed with that specific explanation. He argued that the bright sunset behind him was the dominant light source, that heavy rain obscured much of the eastern horizon, and that the newly rising Moon would have been low and comparatively inconspicuous. His later interview consistently emphasised sunlight rather than moonlight as the illumination responsible for the appearance of the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.thinkaboutitdocs.com/1960-the-cressy-cigar/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thinkaboutitdocs.com">[Think About It Docs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thinkaboutitdocs.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1960 the cressy cigar</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Think About It Docs1960: The Cressy CigarJuly 7, 2013...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 7, 2013</span></span></span>

That disagreement highlights an important distinction. Rejecting the Moon as the principal light source does not automatically eliminate a weather explanation. The same meteorological conditionslow scud cloud, rain curtains and dramatic sunset lightingremain capable of producing unusual visual impressions even if the illumination came primarily from the setting Sun.

## How Strong Is the Weather Theory?

Among conventional explanations, storm-related cloud remains one of the most plausible because it matches the known environmental conditions at the time of the sighting. It naturally explains why the objects emerged from rain, why they disappeared back into it, why estimating distance was difficult and why the observation occurred during rapidly changing evening light.

Its weakness is that it does not fully reproduce the Brownings' more detailed descriptions, particularly the apparently coordinated movement of multiple smaller objects and the structured appearance they attributed to the larger form.

For that reason, the weather hypothesis is best viewed as a partial explanation rather than a complete solution. It significantly reduces the need to invoke an extraordinary object by showing how an unstable rain squall could generate misleading visual impressions, but it cannot confidently account for every reported detail. Within Tasmania's UFO history, the Cressy case therefore remains an example of a sighting where the environmental conditions strongly favour a natural interpretation, even though the surviving witness testimony leaves some aspects unresolved.
