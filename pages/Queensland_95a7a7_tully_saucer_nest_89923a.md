## Introduction

The Tully [saucer nest]({{ 'saucer-nest/' | relative_url }}) became [Queensland]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-queenslands-ufo/' | relative_url }})’s best-known UFO case because it combined a close-range witness report with a large physical disturbance that police, the Royal Australian Air Force and civilian investigators could inspect. On 19 January 1966, banana grower George Pedley said he saw a grey, disc-like object rise near Horseshoe Lagoon at Euramo, south of Tully. In the lagoon he found an oval or near-circular area of uprooted reeds, later floating as a tightly arranged mat roughly nine metres across.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Queensland_95a7a7_tully_saucer_nest_89923a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Tully Nest" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The formation was real, and nothing in the surviving record demonstrates that Pedley invented his experience. What the evidence does not establish is that an unknown craft landed in the lagoon or produced the reeds’ unusual arrangement. Plant tests found no extraordinary material or radiation effects, investigators recorded no burn damage or mechanical debris, and the official inquiry regarded localised turbulence, a waterspout or another wet-season wind phenomenon as a plausible cause. The fairest verdict is therefore unresolved at the level of Pedley’s sighting, but unsupported as proof of a spacecraft landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

## What George Pedley said happened

At about 9 am, Pedley was driving a tractor along a track near Horseshoe Lagoon on Albert Pennisi’s cane farm. He heard a loud hissing noise above the tractor engine and initially suspected a leaking tyre. He then reported seeing a dull grey or blue-grey object near treetop height, shaped like two saucers placed face to face. He estimated it at about 25 feet across and eight or nine feet deep. According to the early official account, it rose vertically, tilted or climbed at an angle and rapidly disappeared towards the south-west.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/queenslandstatearchives/50704411782" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentifiable Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentifiable Flying Objects - Page 29 &#124; Transcription: SUN… &#124; Flickr...</span></span></span>

Pedley’s view was brief. The RAAF summary gave a duration of about 15 seconds, while later retellings have ranged from only a few seconds to roughly half a minute. This variation is not necessarily evidence of dishonesty: estimates made after a startling, fast-moving event are often imprecise. It does, however, limit how much confidence can be placed in his judgements of the object’s size, distance and speed. He reported no portholes, occupants, antennae or other fine structural features that might have distinguished a solid machine from an unfamiliar airborne mass.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

There is also a subtle but important difference between saying the object rose **from the lagoon** and saying it was first seen above or beside it. Newspaper accounts quickly adopted the more dramatic image of a saucer emerging directly from the swamp. In a later interview, Pedley reportedly disputed the strongest version of that wording, saying there had been a misunderstanding and that he had not claimed to see it come out of the water itself. That weakens any simple assertion that he directly watched a craft create the nest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

After the object disappeared, Pedley approached the lagoon. In the earliest reconstruction, he saw a clear patch of slowly rotating water where reeds had previously been growing. When he returned later, the opening was covered by a floating mat of uprooted vegetation. The reeds were described as arranged radially, with their stems following a curved pattern. Pedley informed Pennisi and eventually reported the matter to Tully police at about 7.30 that evening. Sergeant A. V. Moylan accompanied him to the lagoon early the next morning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

This reporting sequence supports Pedley’s sincerity better than it supports his interpretation. He did not begin with a prepared publicity campaign, and people who knew him later described him as distressed by the ridicule that followed. Yet sincerity cannot by itself identify what he saw. An honest witness can accurately report an unusual experience while being mistaken about its cause, scale or relationship to a nearby ground disturbance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

## What was actually found in the lagoon

The strongest evidence in the case is not the description of the object but the existence of a conspicuous reed formation. Contemporary photographs and police-linked records show a large mat of flattened or uprooted swamp vegetation. Official measurements described the affected area as approximately 32 feet by 25 feet, making it somewhat oval rather than a geometrically perfect circle. Newspaper coverage usually rounded this to about 30 feet, or nine metres, across.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[abc.net.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

Accounts agree on several basic features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Reeds had been displaced over a sharply defined area.
* Much of the vegetation was floating rather than simply bent in place.
* The stems formed an organised curved or radial pattern.
* Surrounding reeds remained standing.
* No obvious vehicle track led through the scrub to the formation.
* Investigators found no clear scorching, combustion smell or machinery fragments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/queenslandstatearchives/50704411782" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentifiable Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentifiable Flying Objects - Page 29 &#124; Transcription: SUN… &#124; Flickr...</span></span></span>

</div>

Those observations make the nest more interesting than an ordinary patch of storm-flattened grass. They do not, however, amount to an imprint whose shape can reliably be matched to Pedley’s object. Later enthusiasts described an outer depressed rim and a central raised or indented portion resembling the underside of a saucer, but this interpretation depends heavily on how the tangled floating vegetation is read in photographs. No precise three-dimensional survey was made before visitors climbed over the formation and damaged the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

The direction in which the reeds were arranged has also generated unnecessary confusion. The original nest is usually described in official summaries as clockwise. Sergeant Moylan’s report apparently first contained “anti-clockwise” before that wording was corrected to clockwise. Other nests subsequently found in the lagoon were arranged in different directions. Later accounts sometimes transferred descriptions from those secondary formations to the original, creating contradictory versions of what investigators saw.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

The vegetation reportedly changed from green to brown unusually quickly. Civilian investigators treated this as potentially significant, arguing that manually uprooted reeds normally took several days to discolour. The RAAF-associated testing instead concluded that the plant material had died through ordinary submersion or natural causes. Both the official and civilian examinations found no radiation effect, and no published laboratory result identified heat damage, exotic chemicals or another signature of unusual propulsion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.slq.qld.gov.au/blog/truth-out-there-queensland-ufo-related-periodicals" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slq.qld.gov.au">[slq.qld.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slq.qld.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Truth Is Out There</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Queensland UFO related periodicals &#124; State Library of Queensland...</span></span></span>

That distinction is central to the case. The nest was unusual in appearance, but “unusual” is not the same as physically anomalous. There was no recovered metal, residue, measurable radiation, magnetic effect or verified biological change tying it to a manufactured flying object.

## What police and the RAAF concluded

Sergeant Moylan inspected the site with Pedley on 20 January and arranged for an official aerial-sighting questionnaire and reed samples to be sent through RAAF channels. His initial thinking included a small helicopter, partly because Pedley described something rising and departing from low level. Moylan also recognised a problem with that theory: there was safer cleared ground nearby, and a helicopter operating close to trees and dense swamp would have been taking an unnecessary risk. Checks reported through RAAF Garbutt found no known military or civil aircraft operating in the area at the relevant time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

The absence of identified aircraft removes one straightforward explanation, but it does not prove that no helicopter or other aircraft was present. Such checks depended on available flight and operational records, not continuous radar or photographic coverage of Horseshoe Lagoon. More importantly, investigators did not find the kinds of collateral evidence expected from a helicopter landing in reeds: no verified rotor damage pattern, landing marks, fuel traces or independent witnesses to an aircraft.

The RAAF’s preferred explanation was meteorological. Operational Command suggested that the depressions had been caused by small, isolated waterspouts. In a February 1966 response, Department of Air secretary A. B. McFarlane said investigation of the area, interviews and testing had revealed nothing significant. He reported that local residents considered similar nests fairly common near the beginning of the wet season and that the University of Queensland found nothing unnatural in the submitted samples. Severe turbulence associated with line squalls, thunderstorms, downdraughts, small whirlwinds and waterspouts was therefore accepted as a possible cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

This was a possibility, not a demonstrated reconstruction. No investigator observed a vortex forming an identical mat at Horseshoe Lagoon, and the weather at the moment of Pedley’s sighting was reported as clear, hot and relatively calm. The official theory relied on what such tropical air movements were capable of doing, together with local testimony that comparable reed circles occurred naturally, rather than on evidence that a storm cell or waterspout had been measured over the lagoon that morning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

A later Department of Defence explanation went further. In 1973, an official letter suggested that a “willy-willy”, meaning a small rotating wind column, may have flattened the vegetation and lifted reeds or debris into the air. From Pedley’s position, this airborne mass might have resembled a grey saucer before moving away and dispersing. Hissing and a circular reed pattern were cited as supporting features. This provides a single conventional mechanism for both the visual sighting and the nest, but it remains speculative because no debris cloud was photographed and Pedley insisted that he had seen a definite, symmetrical object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

## How strong are the natural explanations?

### Localised wind or a waterspout

A small vortex remains the most economical explanation for the physical formation. Rotating air over shallow water can move loose vegetation, pull plants from soft sediment and deposit material in a curved pattern. Tully’s tropical wet-season environment makes intense localised air movements more plausible than they would be in a dry, stable setting. The reported rotating water, displaced reeds and lack of heat damage are broadly compatible with such a mechanism.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

The weaknesses are equally clear. The morning was described as fine, no one documented a storm or vortex at the lagoon, and the nest’s sharply bounded appearance was more orderly than many readers would expect from random weather damage. A vortex also does not automatically explain Pedley’s impression of a solid double-convex object. The theory works best if his sighting was a short-lived mass of spray, mist and vegetation whose form and distance he misjudged.

Reports of similar reed circles nearby strengthen the natural explanation, although they are not conclusive. An electrician told police that he had seen comparable formations at Kurrimine Beach and believed they were made by whirlwinds drawing up water and uprooting grass. Officials also said local residents regarded such nests as fairly common at the onset of the wet season. Unfortunately, those comparison formations were not documented to the same standard, so their resemblance cannot now be independently tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer Nest...</span></span></span>

### A helicopter or conventional aircraft

A helicopter can produce circular or spiral disturbance through rotor wash, and it could account for an object rising vertically with a loud rushing noise. Yet the site was awkward, no matching flight was identified, and no landing-gear marks or other confirming traces were recorded. Reports differed over the direction in which helicopter rotors would have driven the reeds, but that argument is less decisive than it appears because water, vegetation and airflow interact too irregularly for rotation direction alone to identify or exclude an aircraft.

The conventional-aircraft hypothesis is therefore possible in the abstract but poorly supported in this case. It requires an unrecorded low-level operation in a hazardous position without leaving persuasive mechanical evidence.

### Animals, decay or ordinary vegetation collapse

Ducks, insects, decaying root systems and floating vegetation have all been mentioned in later retellings. Such processes might create openings or mats in swamp growth, and the discovery of additional formations without associated UFO sightings shows that a “nest” did not necessarily require a flying object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

These suggestions are weaker as complete explanations for the first formation. No investigation identified insect damage or animal activity capable of arranging a large quantity of reeds so neatly within the reported time window. They are more useful as a reminder that wetland vegetation can detach, float, rotate and gather in unexpected ways than as a solved mechanism for the exact 1966 pattern.

### A deliberate hoax

No strong evidence shows that Pedley or the Pennisi family manufactured the original nest. The formation was in deep, unpleasant swamp water; there was no demonstrated construction method; and the publicity brought property damage, ridicule and unwanted visitors rather than an obvious benefit. Hundreds of sightseers soon trampled the farm, making the family’s daily life difficult. Pedley reportedly avoided publicity in later life because of the mockery he endured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains famous but not proven

Tully survives in UFO history because several normally separate elements occurred together: a named witness, a report made on the same day, police inspection, photographs, physical samples and official correspondence. Queensland State Archives preserves contemporary press material and police-related records, while National Archives material records the RAAF’s handling of Pedley’s report. This gives the case a firmer historical foundation than stories reconstructed only decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flickr.com/photos/queenslandstatearchives/50704411782" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flickr.com">[Flickr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flickr.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Unidentifiable Flying Objects</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Unidentifiable Flying Objects - Page 29 &#124; Transcription: SUN… &#124; Flickr...</span></span></span>

Yet the investigation was not designed like a modern forensic inquiry. The site was not secured. Accurate elevations, water movements and plant positions were not mapped before disturbance. Weather conditions immediately above the lagoon were not instrumentally measured. Samples were limited, and the publicly described tests did not establish a detailed chain of physical changes from healthy rooted reeds to the floating brown mat. Heavy visitor traffic soon contaminated or destroyed possible traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

Later reporting has strengthened the human and documentary side of the story. It has confirmed that Pedley was a real local farmer, that the family regarded his distress as genuine, that the reed formation was photographed, and that police and defence authorities did investigate. It has not produced new physical evidence capable of identifying the object or linking it mechanically to the nest. Claims of radiation, burning or technologically unusual plant effects remain unsupported by the available test summaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2024-11-24/sugar-cane-farm-ufo-mystery-expanse-podcast-series-uncropped/104559256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ABC News Tully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsTully&#x27;s cane farm crop circles and an enduring 58yo UFO mystery - ABC News...</span></span></span>

The case is also frequently described as the world’s first crop circle, but that label can mislead. The Tully formation involved uprooted wetland reeds floating on a lagoon, not cereal crops bent into an elaborate design in a dry field. British crop-circle makers Doug Bower and Dave Chorley later said the Tully story helped inspire their hoaxed formations, which increased its cultural influence without making the original lagoon event a hoax or an extraterrestrial landing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.australiangeographic.com.au/blogs/tim-the-yowie-man/2020/09/saucer-hysteria-the-case-of-the-tully-crop-circle/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: australiangeographic.com.au">[Australian Geographic]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">australiangeographic.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Australian Geographic Saucer hysteria: The case of the Tully crop circle</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Australian GeographicSaucer hysteria: The case of the Tully crop circle - Australian Geographic...</span></span></span>

## The most defensible verdict

Three conclusions can be separated with reasonable confidence.

**First, the physical disturbance existed.** Photographs, contemporary reporting and official records establish that an unusually large mat or depression of reeds was present in Horseshoe Lagoon.

**Second, Pedley probably reported an experience he believed was real.** His prompt approach to the property owner and police, his apparent agitation and the lasting personal cost of the publicity provide no obvious basis for dismissing him as a deliberate hoaxer.

**Third, the evidence does not demonstrate a landed flying saucer.** No instrument recorded the object, no second person witnessed its departure, and no tested material showed an extraordinary origin. The timing and causal link between the airborne sighting and the reed formation rest almost entirely on Pedley’s interpretation of a brief event.

A localised wind or water-vortex process is the leading conventional explanation because it fits the setting, the rotating water, the uprooted vegetation and reports of similar wet-season formations. It was never conclusively demonstrated, and it does not reproduce every detail of Pedley’s account. Tully is therefore best classified as a genuine but inadequately measured physical-trace case: unresolved in its precise cause, weakened by gaps and inconsistencies, and far short of evidence that an unknown craft landed in Queensland.
