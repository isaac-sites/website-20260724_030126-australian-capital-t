## Introduction

During the Cold War, unusual aerial sightings reported to Western Australian police did not simply remain in local station files. When officers considered a report sufficiently detailed or credible, it could be forwarded through police headquarters to the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF), which had responsibility for assessing whether an unidentified object might represent an issue for air defence, aviation safety or national security. This administrative pathway is one of the most significant features of [Western Australia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-western/' | relative_url }})'s surviving police UFO archive because it shows how ordinary civilian reports entered official government channels without implying that the reported objects were considered extraterrestrial. Instead, the surviving records reveal a practical reporting system designed to ensure that potentially important aerial observations reached the authorities responsible for Australia's airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/state-records-office-of-western-australia/stories-the-state-archives-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wa.gov.au">[wa.gov.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Western Australian GovernmentStories from the State Archives CollectionDecember 8, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2025</span></span></span>

## From local police station to headquarters

The reporting chain began in much the same way as any other police matter. A member of the public, government employee or other witness attended a local police station or contacted an officer to describe an unusual sighting. Officers recorded the statement, noting practical details such as the time, location, direction of travel, weather conditions and the witness's description of the object.

One distinctive feature of the Western Australian files is that officers often commented on the apparent reliability of the witness. Descriptions such as "reliable", "respectable" or "teetotaller" appear in several reports. These were not endorsements of the sighting itself. Rather, they reflected the police practice of helping senior officials judge whether a report deserved further attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/state-records-office-of-western-australia/stories-the-state-archives-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wa.gov.au">[Western Australian Government]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Western Australian GovernmentStories from the State Archives CollectionDecember 8, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2025</span></span></span>

If a report appeared significant, it was forwarded to Police Headquarters. From there, selected cases could be sent to the RAAF, sometimes accompanied by correspondence requesting technical assessment or additional investigation. The surviving archive therefore documents an administrative chain rather than a separate UFO investigation unit within the WA Police.

## Why the RAAF collected unusual aerial reports

The RAAF's interest reflected Cold War realities rather than an official belief in extraordinary craft.

During the 1950s and 1960s Australia was expanding its air-defence network while monitoring increasingly busy skies that included military aircraft, commercial aviation, rockets, weather balloons and, later, satellites. An unidentified aerial report might prove to be entirely ordinary, but it could also involve an aircraft in distress, foreign military activity, experimental technology or another matter relevant to national defence.

For that reason, reports reaching the RAAF were treated primarily as intelligence or aviation matters. The objective was to determine whether an observation matched known aircraft movements, astronomical objects, missile activity or other identifiable phenomena before considering it unresolved. The process resembled a filtering system rather than an effort to build evidence for extraordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/state-records-office-of-western-australia/stories-the-state-archives-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wa.gov.au">[Western Australian Government]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Western Australian GovernmentStories from the State Archives CollectionDecember 8, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2025</span></span></span>

This broader national approach explains why Western Australian police reports became part of a federal assessment process despite originating as local witness statements.

## The Bridgetown case shows the process in action

The best documented example comes from a sighting near Bridgetown in November 1951.

Charles Aubrey Bolton, a Lands Department inspector, reported seeing an apparently wingless object displaying coloured lights and illuminated openings while camping east of Bridgetown. Local police considered the account sufficiently important to prepare an official report for the Police Commissioner, with a copy forwarded to RAAF Pearce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on com.au.</span></span></span>

The subsequent correspondence illustrates how the reporting chain functioned. Rather than accepting the report at face value, the RAAF requested that police supervise the completion of a detailed witness questionnaire. The form sought precise information including:

* exact time of observation;
* direction and angle above the horizon;
* estimated speed and altitude;
* changes in course or brightness;
* weather conditions;
* duration of the sighting; and
* whether any sound, exhaust or vapour trail was observed.

</div>

The questions reveal what investigators regarded as useful evidence. They were attempting to gather information that could be compared with known aircraft operations or natural explanations rather than relying on dramatic descriptions alone.

Unfortunately, the preserved questionnaire survives with many answer fields left blank, demonstrating one of the recurring frustrations of the archive. The paperwork confirms official follow-up but often preserves too little information to establish what investigators ultimately concluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on com.au.</span></span></span>

## What incomplete records reveal about the process

The surviving files indicate that the reporting system worked as an administrative pipeline, but they also show why modern researchers must be cautious.

Many files contain the initial police report and forwarding correspondence but not the complete investigative outcome. In some cases it is unclear whether the RAAF completed a technical assessment, whether additional witness interviews occurred or whether the matter was quietly closed after routine checks.

This absence of complete documentation should not be mistaken for evidence of suppression. Government record-keeping practices, later archival selection, routine destruction schedules and incomplete file preservation all contribute to gaps in historical collections. The surviving documents demonstrate that reports moved through official channels; they do not demonstrate that every report resulted in a detailed investigation or extraordinary finding.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/state-records-office-of-western-australia/stories-the-state-archives-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wa.gov.au">[Western Australian Government]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Western Australian GovernmentStories from the State Archives CollectionDecember 8, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 8, 2025</span></span></span>

The files also show that credibility assessments had clear limits. A police officer might sincerely believe that a witness was honest, yet honesty alone could not establish an object's speed, distance or identity. Without radar records, photographs, independent corroboration or complete technical analysis, many cases necessarily remained unresolved rather than confirmed.

## Why this reporting chain matters in Western Australia's UFO history

Understanding how reports reached the RAAF helps explain why the Western Australian police archive is historically valuable.

The files were not assembled to prove the existence of unidentified craft. Instead, they preserve evidence that government agencies treated some civilian aerial reports as potentially relevant to defence and aviation responsibilities. The reporting pathway also demonstrates cooperation between state police and federal defence authorities during a period when Australia's security agencies routinely monitored unusual activity in the skies.

For historians, this administrative trail is often more informative than the sightings themselves. It shows how unusual observations were evaluated within government, what information officials considered important, and where the evidential limits lay.     return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
</script>
