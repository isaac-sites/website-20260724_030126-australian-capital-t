## Introduction

The Sea Fury encounter of 31 August 1954 is often described as [New South Wales]({{ 'which-new-south-wales-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }})’s strongest radar-visual UFO case. A Royal Australian Navy pilot returning to Nowra reported two brilliant objects manoeuvring near his aircraft, while personnel at the naval air station saw three radar echoes in the same general area. The incident matters because the pilot and radar operator submitted written reports within days, making it more securely documented than sightings reconstructed only from later memories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The [evidence]({{ 'evidence/' | relative_url }}) does not, however, establish an extraordinary craft. The radar contacts were observed briefly, the Sea Fury was never positively separated from the other echoes by a controlled identifying turn, and no surviving radar plot, technical log or complete intelligence assessment permits a modern reconstruction. Later retellings also added speed estimates and other details that do not appear in the 1954 reports. The fairest judgement is therefore that something unusual was reported and recorded, but its nature remains unresolved.

## The Pilot’s Account and Radar Reports

Lieutenant J. A. “Shamus” O’Farrell was flying a Hawker Sea Fury on a solo night cross-country exercise from the Royal Australian Naval Air Station at Nowra. His planned route ran from Nowra through Young, Temora and Yass before returning to base, at a briefed altitude of 13,000 feet. At 18:58 he reported passing Yass and estimated reaching Nowra at 19:20. After switching from civil air radio to the naval exercise frequency, he contacted Nowra while south-west of Goulburn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

At about 19:10, according to the report he submitted on 2 September, O’Farrell saw a very bright light approaching rapidly from his one o’clock position. It crossed ahead of the Sea Fury and moved to his port side, where it seemed to circle or hold position. He then noticed a second similar light at nine o’clock. This one passed roughly a mile ahead before turning towards the position of the first.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

O’Farrell asked Nowra whether the station had his aircraft on radar because he hoped controllers could confirm that other aircraft were nearby. He was told that the display showed three echoes. The station suggested that he turn through 180 degrees so his own return could be identified. Before any such identification was completed, the two lights appeared together on his left and departed towards the north-east.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The pilot did not describe clearly defined discs or metallic machines. His original wording was considerably more cautious: he could distinguish only a vague shape, with a white light positioned centrally on top. He nevertheless regarded their apparent crossing speed as exceptional. His instrument showed that the Sea Fury itself was travelling at 220 knots, or roughly 407 kilometres per hour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The radar operator’s written account broadly supports the central claim but is less dramatic than many later versions. At 19:07, after aircraft 921 called Nowra, the operator checked the ground-controlled interception display. Two “paints”, meaning radar returns, appeared at a bearing of about 280 degrees and a range of 32 miles from the station. About 15 seconds later, a third appeared in the same area. One return was moving towards Nowra while the other two were travelling north-east.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

About two minutes later, the station asked O’Farrell to make a 180-degree turn if he wanted a bearing and positive identification. The pilot replied “Negative”, and the operator did not continue tracking the returns. This is a crucial limitation. The radar display showed three targets where three targets were expected from the combined visual and radar story, but the station did not conclusively establish which echo belonged to the Sea Fury.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The bearing and range given by the operator place the radar contacts roughly towards Marulan, between Goulburn and Nowra. That is reasonably compatible with O’Farrell’s account of being south-west of Goulburn on the return leg. The timing is not perfectly aligned—19:07 in the operator’s statement versus approximately 19:10 in the pilot’s—but both were recording an operational event rather than producing a forensic reconstruction, so a difference of several minutes is not necessarily significant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

## Why the Case Attracted Official Attention

The report reached the press in December 1954, nearly four months after the flight. Contemporary newspaper coverage stated that radar officers at Nowra had recorded the unidentified aircraft and that Naval Intelligence had referred the matter to Royal Australian Air Force intelligence. The Minister for the Navy, William McMahon’s predecessor William Francis, publicly confirmed the essential outline: a naval pilot flying near Goulburn had seen two lights, had contacted Nowra and had reported objects moving much faster than his Sea Fury.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://trove.nla.gov.au/newspaper/article/96500678" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: trove.nla.gov.au">[Trove]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">trove.nla.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Trove&quot;FLYING SAUCERS&quot; CONFIRMED BY NOWRA RADAR</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Annotate. Official interest should not be mistaken for official endorsement of an extraterrestrial explanation. In the early Cold War, unknown aircraft near defence facilities or military flights were potential intelligence and air-safety concerns regardless of their origin. National Archives of Australia notes that the Commonwealth recorded UFO reports partly because unidentified objects might represent foreign technology or another security issue. Reports from trained defence personnel naturally received more attention than unsupported accounts from members of the public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.naa.gov.au/blog/flying-saucers-fact-or-fiction" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: naa.gov.au">[NAA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">NAAFlying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NAAFlying saucers – fact or fiction? &#124; naa.gov.au</span></span></span>

The surviving papers also contain an assessment that O’Farrell was a credible witness and had been visibly shaken by the event. That supports the conclusion that he sincerely reported what he believed he had seen. It does not show that his estimates of distance, motion or speed were necessarily accurate. Judging the relative movement of isolated lights at night is difficult because their size and range are unknown; a nearby dim object and a distant bright one can produce similar visual impressions.

The case remained of interest to later investigators. American astronomer and UFO consultant J. Allen Hynek interviewed O’Farrell during a visit to Australia in 1973. According to later accounts, O’Farrell was allowed to review Joint Intelligence Bureau material before the interview, and Hynek did not offer a satisfactory identification. Australian researchers subsequently obtained copies of Navy documents, including the pilot’s and operator’s written statements, and located two relevant 1954 files in the National Archives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/kbcat/sea_fury_1954.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">sea fury 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: THE ‘SEA FURY’ RADAR INCIDENT REVISITED...</span></span></span>

There are separate contemporary accounts from the aircraft and the radar station, official correspondence and newspaper reporting from the same year. The National Archives continues to present it as an unresolved incident in which both visual observations and radar returns were recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

## Where the Strongest Claim Falls Short

Calling the incident “radar-confirmed” is reasonable only in a limited sense. Radar personnel confirmed that three returns appeared in the relevant sector while O’Farrell was reporting two lights near his aircraft. They did not demonstrate that the two additional returns corresponded precisely to the lights he saw. Nor did they calculate the objects’ altitude, speed, size or flight path from a preserved radar record.

The failed identification manoeuvre is especially important. Nowra proposed a 180-degree turn so that O’Farrell’s echo could be distinguished by its change of direction. The original reports indicate that he declined, and the radar operator then stopped tracking the targets. Some later retellings instead say that the pilot completed the turn and that radar positively identified his aircraft. That stronger version is not supported by the contemporary statements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

Later accounts also increased the apparent performance of the objects. O’Farrell’s 1954 report says only that their crossing speed seemed faster than anything he had previously experienced. It gives no measured departure speed. By the 1990s, published versions were describing the lights as travelling at perhaps 1,000 miles per hour and the Sea Fury as cruising at 330 knots. The original document records 220 knots and contains no numerical estimate for the objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

Another complication is the presence of a Trans-Australia Airlines Convair flying north at 14,000 feet. Later versions sometimes claimed that O’Farrell was the only aircraft airborne anywhere nearby. The 1954 records instead state that no other Navy or Air Force aircraft were known to be in the area, but that the civilian Convair was present. Contemporary ministerial comments likewise mentioned it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The Convair alone is not an obvious complete explanation. One airliner does not neatly account for two lights apparently moving on different bearings, together with two unidentified radar returns heading north-east. Its route, precise position and visibility from the Sea Fury would have to be established before it could be linked to any part of the observation. Nevertheless, its omission from many popular versions shows how the story became cleaner and more mysterious in retelling.

## Plausible Explanations and Their Problems

No conventional explanation has been demonstrated from the available evidence. Several broad possibilities remain open, each with shortcomings.

**Known aircraft** are the most straightforward possibility. The TAA Convair may have produced one visual or radar contact, while another aircraft might have been operating without its details reaching the investigators whose surviving papers are now available. Secret or poorly coordinated military flying cannot be ruled out simply because local Navy staff reported no known service aircraft nearby. One later researcher proposed early Australian Sabre jet activity as a possible explanation, but the theory is circumstantial and lacks flight records placing two Sabres near Goulburn at the required time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/kbcat/sea_fury_1954.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">sea fury 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: THE ‘SEA FURY’ RADAR INCIDENT REVISITED...</span></span></span>

**Radar artefacts or propagation effects** could account for one or more returns. Radar systems of the period could display interference, reflections or weather-related anomalous propagation. The operator’s statement is too brief to assess echo strength, stability, altitude data, equipment settings or local atmospheric conditions. Yet a purely radar-based explanation would still leave the pilot’s simultaneous visual observations to be explained separately.

**Celestial objects** are less persuasive as a complete answer because O’Farrell reported rapid relative movement and two lights apparently changing position around his aircraft. Bright stars or planets can seem to move when viewed from a turning aircraft, but the available report does not document the Sea Fury’s attitude and manoeuvres closely enough to test that hypothesis. Celestial objects would not normally produce the corresponding radar paints described by Nowra.

**Misjudged distance and speed** probably affected the pilot’s impressions even if real aircraft were present. Without knowing the objects’ size or range, apparent speed cannot be converted into actual speed. A crossing light may seem extraordinarily fast because it is closer than assumed. This could reduce the need for exotic performance without identifying what produced the lights.

None of these possibilities comfortably explains every reported feature. Equally, the absence of a comfortable ordinary explanation does not establish a non-human craft. The case occupies the genuinely unresolved middle ground: better evidenced than a simple anecdote, but too incomplete for a secure technical conclusion.

## What the Missing Records Prevent Us Knowing

The largest gap is the reported Joint Intelligence Bureau investigation file. O’Farrell apparently saw a substantial defence dossier in 1973, but researchers have not located that material in the publicly available archival collection. It may have contained interviews, radar assessments, checks of civilian and military traffic, weather information or reports from possible ground witnesses. It may also have contained little more than duplicated correspondence. Until it is found, its significance cannot be assumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/kbcat/sea_fury_1954.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">sea fury 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: THE ‘SEA FURY’ RADAR INCIDENT REVISITED...</span></span></span>

The surviving radar operator’s statement does not provide enough information to reconstruct the contacts. There is no known radar photograph, plotting sheet or continuous record showing how long each paint persisted, whether the returns had similar strength, whether they merged with ground clutter, or how accurately their speed and direction were measured. The operator explicitly said the targets were not tracked further after the pilot declined the identifying turn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

The missing material also prevents reliable investigation of reported additional witnesses. Later accounts referred to people near Marulan and Sydney who supposedly saw lights, but their original statements have not emerged alongside the Navy reports. Basic questions remain unanswered: what direction they looked, whether they saw two lights plus the Sea Fury, how long the objects were visible and whether their observations were genuinely independent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

A firm conclusion would require records that tie the separate strands together: complete air-traffic movements, precise radar plots, equipment and weather data, the intelligence investigators’ calculations and original testimony from any ground observers. Without those items, investigators cannot establish whether the radar and visual contacts represented the same two objects, several ordinary aircraft, technical artefacts or some combination of causes.

## How the Case Should Be Judged

The Sea Fury incident deserves its prominent place in New South Wales UFO history because its basic framework is well supported. A naval pilot reported two unusual lights during flight; a radar operator contemporaneously recorded three returns in the relevant sector; written statements were made almost immediately; and the matter passed through naval and air-force intelligence channels. Those facts survive independent of later television reconstructions or sensational summaries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com/2012/11/facts-matter-1954-goulburn-radarvisual_28.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com">[UFOs Scientific Research]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufos-scientificresearch.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UFOs Scientific Research Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFOs Scientific ResearchUnidentified Anomalous Phenomena - scientific research: Facts matter - the 1954 Goulburn radar/visual event - par...</span></span></span>

Its reputation has nevertheless become stronger than its evidence. The radar did not conclusively identify the Sea Fury among the three echoes. The targets were not continuously tracked. No instrument established extraordinary speed. A known civilian aircraft was in the wider area, and details added decades later sometimes conflict with the original documents.

Later archival research therefore both strengthened and weakened the case. It strengthened it by recovering contemporary reports that verify the pilot-radar encounter was real and officially considered. It weakened the most dramatic version by exposing inflated speeds, an uncertain identification manoeuvre and the overlooked Convair. The result is not a debunked incident, but neither is it proof of a technologically extraordinary vehicle.

Within the history of UFO reports in New South Wales, the Sea Fury encounter is best classified as a credible but technically underdetermined radar-visual event. The witnesses and documents justify continued interest. The missing radar and intelligence records prevent anything more definite than “unresolved”.
