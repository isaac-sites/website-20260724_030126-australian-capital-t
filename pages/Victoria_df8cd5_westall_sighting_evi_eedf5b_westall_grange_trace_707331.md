## Introduction

One of the most persistent questions about the 1966 [Westall]({{ 'westall/' | relative_url }}) sighting is whether the object reported over the schools left physical evidence after descending towards The Grange, an area of scrub and farmland south of the school grounds. Some witnesses later recalled circular or flattened patches of grass that appeared to mark where the object had been. These alleged traces have become central to claims that the incident involved more than an unusual object seen in the sky.

However, the available evidence for a genuine landing site is much weaker than the evidence that many people saw something unusual overhead. Reports of ground marks were recorded inconsistently, photographs from the day have not surfaced, and no contemporary scientific examination of the site is known. ## What witnesses reported at The Grange

Many former students recalled running towards The Grange after the object appeared to descend behind the trees. In later interviews, some said they found one or more circular areas where the grass looked flattened, discoloured or otherwise disturbed. A number of witnesses believed these marks corresponded with where the object had briefly rested before taking off again.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blogs.slv.vic.gov.au/our-stories/strange-lights-in-the-sky-the-westall-ufo-event-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogs.slv.vic.gov.au">[State Library Victoria Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogs.slv.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">strange lights in the sky the westall ufo event 1966</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State Library Victoria BlogStrange lights in the sky: The Westall UFO event, 19666 Apr 2024 — On 6 April 1966 something strange happened...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

These inconsistencies do not necessarily mean that witnesses invented the story. They do, however, make it difficult to reconstruct exactly what, if anything, was present on the ground immediately after the sighting.

Another important point is chronology. Many of the most detailed descriptions of the alleged landing traces were recorded decades after the event, when memories had inevitably been influenced by repeated discussion, documentaries, reunions and media coverage. Contemporary newspaper reports concentrated primarily on the aerial sighting and the large number of witnesses rather than providing detailed documentation of physical traces.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blogs.slv.vic.gov.au/our-stories/strange-lights-in-the-sky-the-westall-ufo-event-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogs.slv.vic.gov.au">[State Library Victoria Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogs.slv.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">strange lights in the sky the westall ufo event 1966</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State Library Victoria BlogStrange lights in the sky: The Westall UFO event, 19666 Apr 2024 — On 6 April 1966 something strange happened...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

## Why the circles are difficult to verify

Unlike some well-known UFO landing claims overseas, there is no surviving scientific record showing that The Grange was examined for soil changes, radiation, unusual plant damage or recovered material.

Several factors limit what can be concluded.

First, no authenticated photographs clearly showing the reported circles have become part of the historical record. Although many accounts mention disturbed grass, the absence of dated, verifiable images prevents later investigators from assessing the marks independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westall_UFO" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Westall UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Westall UFO was a reported UFO sighting in Australia that occurred on 6 April 1966 in Melbourne, Victoria. Some witnesses reported se...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

Second, The Grange in 1966 was not an isolated, protected site. It consisted of scrub, paddocks and grazing land regularly used by farmers, livestock and local children. Ordinary activity could produce flattened vegetation or worn patches without any connection to an aerial object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blogs.slv.vic.gov.au/our-stories/strange-lights-in-the-sky-the-westall-ufo-event-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogs.slv.vic.gov.au">[State Library Victoria Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogs.slv.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">strange lights in the sky the westall ufo event 1966</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State Library Victoria BlogStrange lights in the sky: The Westall UFO event, 19666 Apr 2024 — On 6 April 1966 something strange happened...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

Third, there appears to have been no controlled examination immediately after the event. By the time investigators and researchers began collecting detailed testimony, any temporary marks would long since have disappeared. Modern researchers therefore depend almost entirely on witness recollections rather than preserved physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://blogs.slv.vic.gov.au/our-stories/strange-lights-in-the-sky-the-westall-ufo-event-1966/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: blogs.slv.vic.gov.au">[State Library Victoria Blog]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">blogs.slv.vic.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">strange lights in the sky the westall ufo event 1966</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">State Library Victoria BlogStrange lights in the sky: The Westall UFO event, 19666 Apr 2024 — On 6 April 1966 something strange happened...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 1966</span></span></span>

Finally, witness testimony itself evolved over time. Some accounts describe merely flattened grass, while later retellings sometimes mention scorched circles or more dramatic physical effects. Without contemporary documentation, it is impossible to determine whether these differences reflect genuine observation, memory changes or the gradual merging of multiple recollections.

## Natural and human explanations for the marks

Because the reported circles cannot be independently verified, several ordinary [explanations]({{ 'explanations/' | relative_url }}) remain plausible.

**Flattened vegetation from normal use.** Grazing cattle, people walking through the area, vehicle tyres or repeated foot traffic could all create roughly circular or irregular flattened patches in grass. On farmland, such marks are not unusual.

**Wind effects.** Strong local gusts, small whirlwinds or weather-related flattening can sometimes leave vegetation pressed in recognisable patterns. Such effects are usually temporary and can appear striking without indicating any unusual cause.

**Recent agricultural activity.** Mowing, burning, spraying, stock movement or other routine land management can produce areas that later observers interpret differently, particularly if they are already expecting to find evidence of a landing.

**Expectation and retrospective interpretation.** Once hundreds of students believed an object had descended into The Grange, any unusual patch of vegetation naturally attracted attention. Human observers often interpret ambiguous physical features in ways that fit an already established narrative. This does not imply dishonesty; it is a well-recognised feature of eyewitness perception, especially in emotionally charged situations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-04-06/westall-ufo-mystery-witnesses-want-answers/106126614" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: abc.net.au">[ABC News]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">abc.net.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">westall ufo mystery witnesses want answers</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ABC NewsAfter 60 years, witnesses to Australia&#x27;s biggest UFO...6 Apr 2026 — Flying saucers, balloons or mass hysteria?
