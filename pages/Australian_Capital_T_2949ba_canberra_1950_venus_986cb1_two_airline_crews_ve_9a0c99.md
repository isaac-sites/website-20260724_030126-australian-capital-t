## Introduction

The 1950 Canberra airline sighting is often remembered because the crew initially believed they were following an unknown aerial object before later accepting that Venus was the most likely explanation. An overlooked comparison from the same year helps explain why experienced pilots could reach such different conclusions. In March 1950, another Australian airline crew flying across South Australia also saw striking alternating coloured lights associated with Venus, but they identified the source correctly because they were able to determine its position by astronomical measurement rather than by visual impression alone. ## The Canberra crew's uncertain distance estimates

The Trans-Australia Airlines crew near Canberra encountered a brilliant light before dawn that appeared to alternate between white and red. As they changed heading and altitude, the light seemed to maintain its relative position and even appeared to respond to their manoeuvres. In the darkness, with few visible stars and no obvious ground references, the pilots had no reliable way to estimate how far away the light actually was. A single bright point against a dark sky provides almost no depth cues, making it surprisingly easy to mistake a distant celestial object for something much nearer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

The uncertainty over distance shaped the crew's interpretation. If the light were nearby, its apparent movement could suggest another aircraft or an unknown object. If it were effectively at an infinite distance, however, the same apparent motion could simply result from the aircraft changing course while the planet remained fixed on the horizon. Because the crew could not independently establish its position, they initially treated the sighting as potentially involving an aerial object rather than an astronomical one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

## Porteous's plotted sighting over South Australia

A useful comparison came from an Australian National Airways flight roughly two months earlier. First Officer M. Porteous reported seeing an impressive display of alternating green and red lights while flying between Ceduna and Adelaide on the Perth–Adelaide service shortly before dawn. Visually, the phenomenon was dramatic enough to resemble many contemporary "flying saucer" reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

The crucial difference was that the aircraft's navigator was carrying out celestial navigation at the time. By taking astronomical observations—commonly known as "star shooting"—the crew could plot the exact position of the bright object against the sky. Those measurements showed that the source was Venus. As the planet climbed to about 15 degrees above the horizon, the coloured flashing effect disappeared, consistent with changing atmospheric conditions rather than the behaviour of a nearby aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

Unlike the Canberra crew, Porteous's flight therefore moved quickly from an unusual visual experience to a confident identification supported by measured positional data instead of appearance alone. The coloured lights were real observations, but the object's location could be verified independently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

## Why measured position changed the conclusion

The comparison illustrates an important principle in aviation observations: identifying an unfamiliar light depends far more on establishing its position than on describing its appearance.

Both airline crews reported features that could easily encourage a more exotic interpretation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exceptionally bright light before sunrise;
* alternating red and lighter colours;
* experienced professional observers;
* an initially puzzling visual display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

</div>

What separated the cases was the available evidence. The South Australian crew had an independent astronomical fix that demonstrated the light remained where Venus should be. The Canberra crew had only visual impressions gathered while manoeuvring an aircraft in darkness, leaving distance and position open to misinterpretation until later consideration of the astronomical circumstances.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/11/flying-saucer-over-australia-1950.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[Garage Graphics History]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucer over australia 1950</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Garage Graphics HistoryFlying Saucers over Australia, 1950November 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2023</span></span></span>

This distinction also explains why witness experience alone cannot determine whether an aerial report remains unexplained.
