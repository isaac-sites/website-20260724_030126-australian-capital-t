## Introduction

Remote [Western Australia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-western/' | relative_url }}) offers some of the longest unobstructed sightlines in Australia. That makes distant lights unusually easy to see, but not necessarily easy to identify. Under the right atmospheric conditions, headlights, mine lighting, roadhouses and other ground-based light sources can appear lifted above the horizon, stretched into unusual shapes or separated from their true location. These effects are genuine optical phenomena caused by atmospheric refraction rather than faults in human eyesight. They help explain why some reports of apparently hovering or floating lights in remote Western Australia later prove to have ordinary terrestrial origins, even when witnesses are describing what they genuinely saw. Understanding these horizon mirages adds an important piece to the broader question of why remote Western Australia produces persistent UFO reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bom.gov.au/glossary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bom.gov.au">[Bureau of Meteorology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bom.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span></span></span>

## Can a Road Light Float Above the Horizon?

The short answer is yes. A distant road light or industrial light can appear to float above the horizon if the atmosphere bends its light towards the observer.

The Bureau of Meteorology defines a mirage as an optical illusion produced when light is refracted through layers of air at different temperatures. The key point is that the object itself is real. What changes is the path taken by its light before it reaches the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bom.gov.au/glossary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bom.gov.au">[Bureau of Meteorology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bom.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span></span></span>

The World Meteorological Organization's *International Cloud Atlas* explains that atmospheric refraction can shift the apparent position of distant objects by several degrees. Under some conditions, objects hidden below the geometric horizon become visible through a phenomenon known as **looming**, while others appear suspended above the skyline or vertically distorted. In stronger cases, multiple upright and inverted images may briefly appear.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[International Cloud Atlas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span></span></span>

In remote Western Australia this matters because horizons often extend for tens of kilometres across deserts, salt lakes and open pastoral country. A driver may see only a single isolated light without any surrounding buildings, trees or terrain to reveal its true location.

## How Atmospheric Refraction Shifts Distant Lights

Most daytime drivers recognise the familiar illusion of water lying across a hot road. The same physical process can affect lights after dark.

When air close to the ground differs greatly in temperature from the air above it, its density changes. Because light travels at slightly different speeds through air of different densities, its path bends rather than remaining perfectly straight. Depending on the temperature profile, the bending may raise, lower or distort the apparent position of a distant light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bom.gov.au/glossary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bom.gov.au">[Bureau of Meteorology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bom.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span></span></span>

In Western Australia several conditions encourage these effects:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Strong daytime heating produces intense temperature gradients above roads, gravel plains and desert surfaces.
* Calm evenings can allow stable layers of warm and cool air to develop.
* Extremely long viewing distances mean even small amounts of refraction accumulate over many kilometres.
* Sparse vegetation and low relief leave few nearby landmarks against which to judge a light's true height.

</div>

Rather than producing dramatic "floating cities", the more common result is subtle displacement. A vehicle headlight may appear detached from the road, a mine light may seem suspended over empty country, or a stationary light may appear to shimmer or slowly change altitude.

## Which Road, Mine and Roadhouse Lights Cause Confusion?

Western Australia's interior contains many isolated but powerful artificial light sources.

These include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--checklist" markdown="1">

* headlights from road trains travelling on highways many kilometres away;
* floodlighting at open-cut mines and mineral processing facilities;
* lighting around remote roadhouses and fuel stops;
* exploration camps and industrial compounds;
* railway signals and maintenance vehicles.

</div>

Because the surrounding landscape is often completely dark, these lights can dominate the horizon. If the terrain between observer and source gently rises, the light itself may remain visible while the vehicle, buildings or infrastructure producing it stays hidden below the horizon. Atmospheric refraction can further exaggerate this effect by lifting the apparent image of the light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[International Cloud Atlas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span></span></span>

The result can resemble a bright object hovering over empty land despite originating from ordinary infrastructure.

Mining districts deserve particular attention. Powerful fixed floodlights may remain visible over very long distances, especially during clear, dry nights. Heat shimmer can make these lights appear to pulse, expand or contract without the source itself moving.

## Why These Lights Can Seem Unusual

Several visual effects often occur together.

A distant light may:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* appear brighter than expected because the surrounding landscape is completely dark;
* flicker as turbulent air changes the refractive path;
* seem to rise or sink as atmospheric layers evolve during the evening;
* appear disconnected from the ground because intervening terrain hides its source;
* seem to move slowly if changing refraction alters its apparent position.

</div>

None of these observations necessarily indicate that the witness made an error. Instead, they show how the atmosphere can modify the appearance of a genuine terrestrial light before it reaches the observer. The observation is real; the interpretation of distance, altitude or motion may not be.

## What Weather and Terrain Evidence Can Test a Mirage Claim?

When investigators assess reports of unusual lights near the Western Australian horizon, atmospheric conditions become an important part of the evidence.

Several questions help distinguish a likely mirage from other explanations.

**Was the light close to the horizon?** Refraction effects are strongest along very shallow viewing angles where light travels through the greatest depth of atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[International Cloud Atlas]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span></span></span>

**Was there strong daytime heating followed by a calm evening?** Large temperature differences between air layers favour refractive effects. The Bureau of Meteorology notes that mirages arise when light passes through air masses of differing temperature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.bom.gov.au/glossary" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: bom.gov.au">[Bureau of Meteorology]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">bom.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Bureau of Meteorology Glossary &#124; The Bureau of Meteorology</span></span></span>

**Were there known light sources in the same direction?** Highway traffic, mines, ports, rail facilities or remote settlements should be mapped before assuming an unknown origin.

**Did the light remain in roughly the same bearing?** Stationary bearings are generally more consistent with distant fixed lights or vehicles following long roads than with nearby manoeuvring objects.

**Did the appearance change gradually rather than abruptly?** Slow changes in elevation or shape often match evolving atmospheric conditions rather than rapid physical movement.

Investigators can also compare reported directions with topographic maps. A light apparently floating above a ridge may actually originate from infrastructure beyond it, revealed only because refraction has bent the light over the intervening terrain.

## What This Means for Western Australia's UFO History

Horizon mirages provide a practical explanation for one recurring category of reports without explaining every unusual sighting.

Many historical Western Australian UFO accounts involve isolated lights seen from highways, stations or mining regions where observers faced exceptionally long horizons and minimal visual reference points. In such settings, atmospheric refraction and concealed ground lights offer a plausible mechanism that should always be considered before concluding that an aerial object was present.

This does not imply that every report has been solved. Some sightings involve multiple witnesses, radar information, aircraft observations or behaviours that cannot be evaluated through horizon optics alone. However, when a report consists primarily of a distant bright light apparently hovering above the landscape, especially on hot, calm nights across remote country, mirages and displaced ground lights provide one of the strongest evidence-based explanations available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cloudatlas.wmo.int/en/mirage.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cloudatlas.wmo.int">[wmo.int]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cloudatlas.wmo.int</span><span class="citation-popover-title">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">International Cloud Atlas Mirage &#124; International Cloud Atlas</span></span></span>

    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
