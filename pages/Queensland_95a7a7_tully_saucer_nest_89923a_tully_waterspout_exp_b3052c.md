## Introduction

The weather explanation for the [Tully saucer nest]({{ 'tully-nest/' | relative_url }}) is not that a conventional storm simply flattened the reeds. Rather, investigators proposed that a small, highly localised rotating wind feature—described in official correspondence as a waterspout, "willy willy", down-draught or severe turbulence associated with tropical thunderstorms—might have disturbed the lagoon while also creating the brief visual impression that George Pedley interpreted as a rising object. This became the Royal Australian Air Force's preferred natural explanation, although it was never proven. The evidence shows that such weather phenomena are plausible in [north Queensland]({{ '1961-reports/' | relative_url }})'s wet season, but no observer actually saw a waterspout over Horseshoe Lagoon that morning, and no meteorological reconstruction has conclusively linked one to the nest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

## Why investigators considered waterspouts

The Tully incident occurred during the tropical wet season, when intense thunderstorms, line squalls and rapidly changing air masses are common across north [Queensland]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-queenslands-ufo/' | relative_url }}). Because the disturbed reeds showed no convincing evidence of heat, radiation or mechanical impact from a solid object, official investigators looked first for natural explanations consistent with local weather.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

The RAAF's investigation gathered several observations that pointed towards atmospheric causes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Local residents reportedly said similar reed disturbances were not unknown during the onset of the wet season.
* The University of Queensland advised that the reed samples showed nothing abnormal and suggested severe turbulence associated with seasonal storms could account for the damage.
* Official correspondence concluded that down-draughts, "willy willies" and waterspouts were known to occur in the district and represented plausible mechanisms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

</div>

One supporting anecdote also influenced investigators. An electrician from the nearby district reportedly told officials he had previously seen similar circular reed patterns at Kurrimine Beach which he believed had been created by a whirlwind drawing water upward into a waterspout. Although this was not direct evidence for the Tully event, it suggested that comparable natural formations were considered possible by experienced local observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

## How turbulence could uproot and rotate reeds

The weather theory depends on the behaviour of small rotating vortices rather than broad, straight-line winds.

A compact vortex passing across shallow water could, in principle:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* produce strong upward suction in its centre;
* create circular airflow around the edges;
* pull floating vegetation into a rotating pattern;
* temporarily clear reeds from part of the lagoon surface;
* leave a roughly circular patch once the disturbed vegetation floated back together.

</div>

This mechanism broadly matches several observed features of the Tully nest, including the circular shape, the apparent rotation of the water immediately after the sighting, and the later appearance of a floating mat of uprooted reeds arranged in a radial pattern. The official explanation suggested that if such a vortex developed over a swampy reed bed, it could flatten the vegetation into a circular formation while lifting spray and debris into the air.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

Several years later, the Department of Air's public relations office described what it regarded as the most probable explanation: a circular wind phenomenon flattened the reeds, lifted debris to around 30 feet (about nine metres), and created the appearance of a disc-shaped object before dissipating. The reported hissing noise was also considered compatible with a rapidly rotating vortex.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

Importantly, this was presented as the most likely hypothesis rather than a demonstrated reconstruction of what happened.

## What the weather theory still cannot prove

Although the waterspout explanation remains one of the strongest conventional interpretations, it leaves important questions unanswered.

First, nobody independently observed a waterspout over Horseshoe Lagoon. The proposed vortex was inferred after the event rather than directly witnessed. Weather conditions in the region were certainly favourable for turbulent phenomena, but there is no contemporaneous meteorological record showing that a waterspout formed at the lagoon itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

Second, George Pedley consistently maintained that he saw a distinct object rather than a whirlwind. He was familiar with tropical weather and rejected the suggestion that he had mistaken an ordinary atmospheric phenomenon for a solid craft. While witness confidence cannot establish what was present, it means the official explanation never fully resolved his account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

Third, later discoveries of additional smaller nests in the surrounding area complicated interpretation. Some showed clockwise rotation while others rotated anti-clockwise. These findings demonstrate that circular reed formations could occur more than once, but they do not by themselves identify the mechanism responsible for each formation. Whether they reflected multiple weather events, different local water movements or another process remains uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://project1947.com/kbcat/kbtrace0505.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947: Keith Basterfield - A CATALOGUE OF AUSTRALIAN PHYSICAL TRACE CASESMay 15, 2005...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 15, 2005</span></span></span>

Finally, the theory explains the physical disturbance more readily than the reported sighting. A small vortex could plausibly disturb reeds, produce a hissing sound and lift spray or debris, yet reconstructing exactly how such a phenomenon would appear to an observer for only a few seconds is much more difficult. That gap is why official investigators regarded the weather explanation as plausible rather than conclusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.project1947.com/forum/bctully.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: project1947.com">[Project 1947]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">project1947.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Project 1947PROJECT 1947 - Forum - Bill Chalker - 1966 Tully Saucer NestJanuary 1, 1997...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1997</span></span></span>

Within Queensland's UFO history, the waterspout hypothesis remains the principal natural explanation for the Tully [saucer nest]({{ 'saucer-nest/' | relative_url }}) because it accounts for many of the physical features without requiring an unknown craft.
