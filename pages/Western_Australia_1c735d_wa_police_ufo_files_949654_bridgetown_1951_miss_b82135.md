## Introduction

The Bridgetown sighting of November 1951 occupies a distinctive place in [Western Australia]({{ 'what-really-happened-in-western/' | relative_url }})'s UFO history because it is one of the earliest surviving cases preserved in the Western Australian Police UFO files. Unlike many later reports, it involved a government employee whose account prompted formal correspondence between the WA Police and the Royal Australian Air Force (RAAF). Yet the case is remembered as much for what is missing as for what was reported. The surviving file shows that officials regarded the incident as worthy of investigation, but the detailed follow-up questionnaire that might have clarified crucial details survives only as a blank form. As a result, the Bridgetown case remains historically important while offering little firm evidence about what was actually seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

## What Charles Bolton reported near Bridgetown

On the night of 28 November 1951, Charles Aubrey Bolton, a Lands Department inspector, was camping near Chowerup, about 37 miles (roughly 60 kilometres) east of Bridgetown. At approximately 11.03 pm he observed what he initially believed was an aircraft passing overhead at an estimated altitude of about 2,000 feet.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://documents.theblackvault.com/documents/ufos/australia/PP474-1_5-5-AIR_1567731.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: documents.theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">documents.theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault CONFIDENTIAL None</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultCONFIDENTIAL None &#124; &#124; &#124; &#124; &#124; &#124; &#124;February 18, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 18, 2026</span></span></span>

At the same time, every important estimate—altitude, speed, size and distance—depended entirely on Bolton's visual impressions, with no independent measurements available.

## How police and the RAAF responded

The Bridgetown report illustrates how unusual aerial sightings were handled in Western Australia during the early Cold War. Local police did not dismiss the account outright. Instead, they forwarded an official report to the Police Commissioner, who in turn provided a copy to RAAF Pearce for assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

The RAAF's response is one of the most significant aspects of the case. The officer commanding Western Area described the matter as "of considerable interest" and requested that a police constable supervise Bolton while he completed a detailed questionnaire. The accompanying correspondence also asked that the witness be reminded of the confidential nature of the information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

This reaction is often misunderstood. It does not indicate that the RAAF believed Bolton had witnessed an extraordinary craft. Rather, it reflects standard defence practice at the time. During the early 1950s, unidentified aerial reports could potentially relate to aircraft, military activity or other matters affecting national security. Collecting systematic information before reaching conclusions was therefore entirely consistent with official procedures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://slwa.wa.gov.au/stories/slwa-abc-radio/truth-not-out-there" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slwa.wa.gov.au">[State Library of Western Australia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slwa.wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wa.gov.au.</span></span></span>

The questionnaire itself sought practical observational details, including:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the precise time and location;
* whether the object appeared self-illuminated;
* changes in direction or altitude;
* whether it maintained a straight course;
* the presence of any vapour trail or exhaust;
* additional characteristics that might distinguish an aircraft from another phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

</div>

These questions demonstrate that investigators were attempting to gather evidence that could support or eliminate conventional explanations rather than simply accepting the witness's interpretation.

## Why the missing questionnaire limits the case

The Bridgetown incident remains unresolved largely because the surviving archival record is incomplete.

Police correspondence from January 1952 indicates that Bolton completed the requested questionnaire. However, the version preserved in the State Records Office contains the printed questions but none of the witness's answers. Whether the completed original was lost, removed or never filed with the surviving papers is unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

That missing information creates several important gaps.

Without Bolton's detailed responses, investigators today cannot determine:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* exactly how long the object remained visible;
* whether its apparent speed changed during observation;
* whether atmospheric conditions could have influenced perception;
* whether any stars, planets or aircraft were considered at the time;
* whether Bolton revised or clarified aspects of his original statement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[The West Australian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>

</div>

These are precisely the kinds of details modern investigators use when assessing historical sightings. Their absence means that the original narrative cannot be tested against astronomical data, aviation records or meteorological conditions with the confidence that a complete report would allow.

The surviving paperwork therefore documents an investigation that began seriously but cannot show how it ended.

## What the case tells us about the WA Police UFO files

The Bridgetown report illustrates both the strengths and weaknesses of the Western Australian Police archive.

On the positive side, the file demonstrates that police documented unusual reports carefully, identified witnesses, preserved dates and locations, and communicated with the RAAF when they considered a case significant. The report survives because it formed part of normal government record-keeping rather than later folklore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://slwa.wa.gov.au/stories/slwa-abc-radio/truth-not-out-there" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: slwa.wa.gov.au">[State Library of Western Australia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">slwa.wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on wa.gov.au.</span></span></span>

At the same time, the archive is incomplete. Missing attachments, absent witness statements and incomplete follow-up records appear elsewhere in the collection as well. The Bridgetown case is therefore representative of a broader problem in historical UFO research: surviving administrative records often prove that an investigation occurred without preserving enough evidence to establish what investigators ultimately concluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wa.gov.au/organisation/state-records-office-of-western-australia/stories-the-state-archives-collection" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wa.gov.au">[Western Australian Government]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wa.gov.au</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Western Australian Government Stories from the State Archives Collection</span></span></span>

## Why the Bridgetown case remains unresolved

More than seventy years later, there is no convincing evidence that identifies what Charles Bolton observed. Equally, there is no evidence that the RAAF confirmed an extraordinary object.

The case survives because contemporaneous officials regarded it as credible enough to investigate, not because they verified Bolton's interpretation. The witness appears to have been treated as reliable, but witness reliability is different from objective identification. A sincere observer can still misjudge distance, size, speed or the nature of an unfamiliar object seen at night.

For historians of Western Australia's UFO records, that distinction is what makes the Bridgetown incident significant. It is one of the state's earliest documented official UFO investigations, yet its most important evidential document—the completed questionnaire that might have resolved key uncertainties—is missing. That absence leaves the sighting suspended between historical fact and historical mystery: the report itself is well documented, but the evidence needed to explain it has not survived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://thewest.com.au/news/wa/was-ufo-history-revealed-state-records-show-the-skies-over-wa-have-seen-many-unidentified-flying-objects-c-8450517" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: thewest.com.au">[com.au]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">thewest.com.au</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The West AustralianUntold history of WA: State records show the skies over WA have seen many UFOs &#124; The West AustralianNovember 5, 2022...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 5, 2022</span></span></span>
